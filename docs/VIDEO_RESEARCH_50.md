# 50-video crowd-footage research catalogue

Researched 10 October 2026. This reaches the **discovery baseline**, not a training or accuracy milestone.

27 concert/festival videos, 12 sports-crowd videos, 7 procession/protest videos, 3 political-gathering videos and 1 controlled drill. These are 50 distinct URLs; independent event count is not yet established.

## What was checked

Search metadata, source identity where available, broad scenario relevance and selected licence pages. Follow-up browser sampling covered 8 of these 50: see [preliminary visual findings](VIDEO_VISUAL_REVIEW.md). The other 42 remain metadata-only. None has completed full visual review, download, annotation or training approval. Stock-video countries remain unverified. Named concert sources cover UK, Belgium, Brazil and Germany; the drill is from the USA and political sources from India.

## Rights correction

This catalogue supersedes the approval/risk-label claims in the earlier 10-source workbook. Pexels' [current terms](https://www.pexels.com/terms-of-service/) restrict unauthorised ML data collection and systematic copying. A free-download badge is not ML dataset permission. Do not bulk download these candidates. News, BBC and official festival videos also need explicit permission or a suitable licence before training use. [The GPD drill page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:GPD_RIOT_DRILL_2009.webm) states CC BY 3.0 with SOFISINTOWN attribution; it still needs technical review.

## Review and preparation

1. Clear reuse permission before acquiring footage for training. Record the permission, source, author and licence date.
2. Watch each candidate. Prefer an uninterrupted real-time wide crowd view. Reject photo montages, stage-only shots and heavily obscured views. Slow motion and timelapse cannot supply real-world speed features without reliable timing correction.
3. Record original resolution/FPS, usable start/end times, camera movement, crowd region and whether it duplicates another event. Stabilise moving-camera footage or exclude it from motion-feature training.
4. Annotate observable behaviour and uncertainty in short windows. Concerts, cheering, political gatherings and high density are not automatically high risk. Distinguish routine motion, observable congestion and loss-of-control evidence; do not claim hidden crowd pressure is measured from video.
5. Group all clips of the same event across sources. Split training/validation/test by event and camera, not adjacent frames. Keep held-out scenes for false-alarm and missed-event evaluation.
6. Supplement these mainly routine-crowd candidates with rights-cleared evacuation/anomalous-flow data. UMN remains a separate staged escape benchmark, not real stampede truth. The Evacuation Videos Database is a dataset source, not one individual video, and is not counted here.

There is no established guarantee that 50 videos makes a reliable model. This collection is biased toward routine crowds and lacks reviewed high-risk/onset examples. It supports dataset planning, not a validated life-safety alarm.
